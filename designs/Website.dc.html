01 — WHY TRAMUS

Why Tramus

01

Free, with no licence keys.

No licence fees, dongles or activation servers. Install it on every lab computer and every student's laptop.

02

Your videos never leave your machine.

Videos are opened read-only and analysed locally. Nothing is uploaded. There's no account and no telemetry.

03

No training, no GPU.

Classic computer vision instead of deep learning: no labelled frames, no model training, no graphics card. An ordinary laptop is enough.

04

Every number is traceable.

The setup, the raw tracking and every manual correction are saved next to the video. Exports include the parameters and app version that produced them.

04 — FEATURES

Features

SETUP

  • Arena and zones: rectangles, ellipses, polygons, 8 zone colours that stay distinct on greyscale video and for colour-blind users.
  • Measure tool: draw a known distance once, then work in cm.
  • "What the tracker sees" preview, with sensitivity, polarity (dark or light mouse) and minimum size, updated live.
  • Trial window and excluded periods with notes. Changing them never requires re-tracking.
  • Templates for whole series of recordings.

REVIEW

  • Playback from 0.25× to 16×. Every frame plays at 1×.
  • A red movement trail and a recent-path heat overlay.
  • Automatic flags: mouse lost, sudden jump, unusual size.
  • Keyboard-first: A accept, R reopen, [ ] previous/next flag, P set position, N not visible, F fill in between.
  • Undo and redo, and a full edit history.

RESULTS

  • Time per zone in seconds and as a share of analysed time, plus "No zone" and "Not visible".
  • Every visit with enter and exit times, in video time and clock time.
  • Zone crossings: count the from→to rules you define (e.g. Left → Center, or "any zone"), plus a full from × to matrix.
  • Distance travelled (with a scale set).
  • Excel or CSV export, wide (one row per video) or long, including a per-frame track sheet.

UNDER THE HOOD

  • Batch tracking in parallel (up to 16 videos at once).
  • Variable-frame-rate aware: timing comes from each frame's timestamp.
  • Engine usable from the command line when run from source (tramus-engine track, tramus-engine export).
  • Tracker plugins: add your own tracking method as a Python package.
05 — METHOD

How it works

No black box. Tramus uses classic, inspectable computer vision. Here is the whole method.

  1. 01 · EMPTY ARENA

    The median of 60 frames sampled across the video. The moving mouse disappears from it.

  2. 02 · DIFFERENCE

    Each frame is compared with the empty arena, inside the arena only. Whatever is darker (or lighter) is a candidate.

  3. 03 · CLEAN-UP

    Morphological filtering removes noise and the thin tail.

  4. 04 · BODY

    The largest blob becomes an ellipse with a position, size and orientation. Gaps of up to 0.5 s are filled; longer ones are flagged for review.

  5. 05 · IN A ZONE?

    96 points are spread across the body ellipse. The mouse is in a zone when at least 50% of its body is inside (configurable). It stays there until 50% is in another zone or on open floor, so doorways don't cause flicker.

Real timestamps.

Lab recordings often have a variable frame rate, so Tramus never assumes a fixed fps. Durations come from each frame's own timestamp. Clock times come from the video's metadata, stored in UTC and shown in your time zone.

Corrections on top, never instead.

The raw tracking is kept untouched. Your fixes are stored as an ordered log on top of it.

Exclusions only cut analysis.

The whole video stays tracked, so you can change excluded periods at any time without re-tracking. A crossing never counts across an excluded period.

07 — PRIVACY & SECURITY

Built to stay on your computer.

Written so you can forward this section to your IT or data-protection office.

WHAT TRAMUS DOES

  • Processes videos locally. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Makes no network connections of its own: no telemetry, analytics, auto-updater, crash reporter or account.
  • Opens videos read-only and never modifies or copies them.
  • Its internal engine (a local Python process) listens only on 127.0.0.1 and requires a random access token generated at every launch.
  • Runs the interface sandboxed (context isolation and sandbox on, no Node.js in the page) with a strict Content-Security-Policy that blocks remote scripts, styles and fonts.
  • Stops the engine and its worker processes when you quit.

WHERE YOUR DATA LIVES

  • <video>.tramus.json next to each video: setup, tracking, corrections and results. It contains the video's file name, not its full path.
  • Exports (.xlsx / .csv) only where you save them.
  • Settings, templates and batch records live in your user config folder (~/Library/Application Support/Tramus on macOS). They include recent folder paths, and templates note which OS user created them.
  • Plain, unencrypted files. Protect and share them like any other research data. File names often contain animal or subject IDs.

09 — LIMITS

What Tramus doesn't do (yet)

  • —One mouse per video.
  • —Top-down, recorded video only. No live camera input, no hardware triggers, no closed-loop control.
  • —Steady conditions. Needs a fixed camera, a fixed arena and steady lighting, with a mouse visibly darker (or lighter) than the floor.
  • —Body position, not posture. No nose or head direction, so object exploration can only be approximated with zones around the objects.
  • —Current measures: time in zone, visits, crossings and distance. No speed, latency-to-first-entry or behaviour classification (rearing, grooming) yet.
  • —Single linear scale, with no lens-distortion correction.
  • —Not code-signed yet, so expect a security prompt on first launch.

11 — FAQ

Questions

Is Tramus really free?+

Yes. It's MIT-licensed open-source software: free for academic and commercial use, with no licence keys and no feature tiers.

Do I need a GPU or a powerful computer?+

No. Tracking runs on the CPU. A recent laptop processes typical recordings many times faster than real time.

Does Tramus need internet access?+

No. Its own code makes no network connections, so you can use it on an offline lab computer.

Which video formats work?+

Common formats: .mp4 .mov .avi .mkv .m4v .wmv .mpg .mpeg, decoded with FFmpeg. Variable frame rates are handled.

How accurate is it?+

Validation is in progress (see Validation & quality). Early comparisons with an established commercial system look close. Until results are published, check a sample of your results manually.

Can it track several mice, or body parts like the nose?+

Not at the moment. Tramus tracks one mouse and its body outline.

Can I fix tracking mistakes?+

Yes. Flagged frames are listed for review. You can set positions, mark the mouse as not visible, or fill gaps. Corrections are logged, undoable and included in exports.

What happens to a crossing if the mouse disappears for a moment?+

Time out of sight or on open floor doesn't break a crossing: Left → (not visible) → Center still counts as Left → Center. An excluded period does break it.

Can I analyse a whole experiment at once?+

Yes. Save the setup as a template, apply it to a batch of videos, track them in parallel, then review each one.
